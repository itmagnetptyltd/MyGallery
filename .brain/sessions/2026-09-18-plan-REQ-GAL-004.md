## What must be true

1. Activating a Thumbnail shows **that same Photo** in the Larger view.
2. The Photo is **rendered larger** than its Thumbnail.
3. **Escape** closes it and the Gallery is shown again.
4. The **close control** does the same.
5. After closing, the Gallery is **at the scroll position it was at**.

## Approach

The Larger view is a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, not a
hand-built overlay. That choice does most of the work the criteria describe:
the browser gives Escape-to-close (criterion 3), a focus trap and a backdrop
for free, and because a modal dialog does not navigate, the Gallery underneath
keeps its scroll position (criterion 5) rather than needing it saved and
restored. A hand-rolled overlay would mean re-implementing all of that, badly.

The dialog shows the **Photo itself**, served by a new
`GET /api/photos/<id>` returning the stored bytes. Two alternatives were
available and both were rejected for this slice: upscaling the stored Thumbnail
would make criterion 2 technically pass while showing the user a blurry 320px
image, and generating a third mid-size rendition would invent storage no
requirement asks for. Serving the full Photo costs nothing here because the
application is on `127.0.0.1` — there is no network between the file and the
browser.

## Decisions this forces

**None that warrant an ADR.** Both real choices here — the native `<dialog>`
over a hand-built overlay, and serving the full Photo over a mid-size rendition
— are cheap to reverse: each is confined to one template, one route and one
block of `app.js`. Recording them would be manufacturing ADRs to look diligent,
which puts noise in the one directory that has to stay trustworthy. They are
written into the Approach above instead.

**If that judgement is wrong**, the one to promote is serving the full Photo:
it becomes expensive the day MyGallery is reachable from another device, which
`REQ-GAL-011` currently forbids.

## What I am unsure about

- **The new route is HTTP surface, and `REQ-GAL-004` declares belts A and B.**
  I have put its tests in `tests/` rather than `tests/api/`, because the
  requirement's `belts:` is the contract `/close-slice` checks and I should not
  quietly widen it. If you would rather the route were proven at belt C, that
  is a change to `REQ-GAL-004`'s `belts:` — a requirement edit, and yours to
  make, not something `/tdd` may do on its way past.
- **Criterion 2 has the same edge as the Thumbnail one.** A Photo smaller than
  the Thumbnail's rendered box may not display larger. The criterion does not
  bound the Photo's size, so I intend to test it with a Photo comfortably larger
  than 320px and say plainly that tiny Photos are outside what it promises —
  the same shape of gap recorded for `REQ-GAL-003` last session.
- **Criterion 5 should be satisfied by `showModal()` doing nothing to scroll.**
  I have not verified that Chromium keeps `window.scrollY` across a modal open
  and close; if it does not, the fix is to save and restore it explicitly, and
  the test stays as written either way.
- **Native `<dialog>` closes on Escape without any JavaScript**, which means
  criterion 3 could pass with no code of ours. That is a real pass — the
  behaviour the client asked for is present — but it is worth knowing the test
  is proving the browser's behaviour as much as ours.
- **Focus handling is not in any criterion.** `showModal()` traps focus and
  returns it on close, which is why the native element was chosen; I am not
  adding aria wiring beyond what the element gives, because no requirement asks
  for it and inventing accessibility scope here is as wrong as skipping it
  would be if it had been asked for.
